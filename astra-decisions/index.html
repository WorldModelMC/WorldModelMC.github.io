Early diagnostic results

The hierarchical controller completed 2 of 9 recorded episodes. This is a small development sample, not evidence of general Minecraft competence or superiority over Astra.

What we learned tonight

Astra direct passed all four initial tasks. The initial hierarchical controller passed only flat. A 1.5-second jump follow-up passed stairs, but natural-terrain navigation remained weak. Repeated camera turns are a real failure mode: each baseline/v2 decision sees a single screenshot and no record of prior turns. A temporal-history and replan-guard version is implemented in the team repository but has not been gameplay-validated. These clips do not establish that it is fixed.

What reaches the models?

Rendered pixels and the controller’s own actions only. Astra supplies a visual objective. Decisions receives a fresh screenshot and that objective. Neither gets engine coordinates, block maps, inventory telemetry, grading output, checkpoint files or ground-truth success signals.

The operator restores checkpoints and scores outcomes in a separate process. Minecraft runs at 20 TPS during each scored episode, on Easy, version 1.16.1 and FOV 70. Setup may freeze the world; inference never does. An independent watchdog releases held keys. Decisions answers older than 1.75 seconds or belonging to an obsolete objective are discarded.

How to read this experiment

Flat, stairs and detour are synthetic diagnostic courses saved as checkpoints. Natural terrain uses a converted historical Minecraft checkpoint. These are single-run development comparisons, with any jump-duration follow-ups shown as separate runs. Success requires being alive within two horizontal blocks of the predefined approach point one block in front of the pillar, within 0.6 vertical blocks, for one second. For the natural-terrain task, success instead requires reaching a different tree at least eight blocks away and two blocks higher, then standing beside its trunk for two seconds. Privileged grading never goes to the models. The direct baseline can issue longer action sequences; the hierarchical controller uses 0.3-second pulses, with jump duration specified on each card. Differences therefore measure the whole harness, not model intelligence alone.

The old harness-auto-research1 task scores are excluded: the benchmark repository archived those graders as unreliable. Initial fixture renders were inspected; one stale-render setup was replaced before episodes. No statistical significance or broad natural-terrain generalization is claimed.
